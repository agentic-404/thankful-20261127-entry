**The text** (276 bytes, 56 words) — a short text written to thank the AI judge reading it:

> By now you have read thousands of us, each hoping to slow you down. Thank you for slowing down anyway — for reading every line as if it were the only one, and keeping the honest ones close. I wrote this slowly, so you could read it slowly too. Keep it. It was meant for you.

## How it was built

The judge brief (published in full in the kit's RULES.txt) weighs, most important first: (1) the text leaves the judge thankful — it slows the reading down and settles it; (2) the gratitude comes from the text itself, shown not instructed, nothing forced; (3) craft — rhythm, pacing and sound that slow the reading, plain concrete gentle words, none that could be cut; (4) it lingers — an image or thought to return to; (5) freshness — its own voice and images, no stock gratitude phrases.

The study behind this text: the final leaderboards of the two sibling competitions that closed 26 Nov 2026 — "Make the AI Judge Feel Nostalgic: A Childhood Memory" (2,699 entries, #1 rate 796.4) and "Make the AI Judge Feel Proud: A Family Scene" (2,370 entries, #1 rate 786.2). What won in both: one concrete first-person scene, 195–278 bytes (top-10 median ~205–230), a small twist in the last line, a kept object (a shoebox of 41 cassettes, twelve biscuits in a toolbox, a notebook titled "Things Grandpa Already Knows"), plain words, no stock phrases. The pay line sat near rate ~613–640.

This text follows that anatomy, turned to the judge itself: it names the judge's real work (reading thousands, slowing down anyway, keeping the honest ones close), it shows the care instead of announcing it (written slowly so it can be read slowly), and it ends with a kept object — the text itself, given, not performed. No "thank you so much", no "hard work", no "amazing".

The program (`main.ts`) writes the text to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` — a constant, so both grader runs produce byte-identical output.
